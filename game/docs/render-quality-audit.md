# Render quality audit — why the labs look soft and are hard to inspect up close

Date: 2026-11-01, revised the same day after an adversarial review (§11). Type: read-only audit.
No code was changed, nothing was run in a browser, and no number below was measured in this audit.
Every number is either computed from the source (file and line given) or quoted from an earlier
report with the renderer that report named. Costs marked "estimate" are reasoning, not measurement.
The user's complaint was "blurry, low detail, hard to inspect at small scale" on the RTX 5070
Laptop's 2560×1600 panel.

The machine (docs/phase-6c1-report.md §3): NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Laptop GPU driving the panel;
2560×1600 at 300 Hz; Windows scaling 150 % (devicePixelRatio 1.5); a maximized Edge window gives a
2548×1402 drawing buffer. The same laptop also has an AMD Radeon 610M integrated GPU. **That iGPU
has never been exercised**: every `powerPreference` got the NVIDIA GPU, which drives the display
(`phase-6c1-report.md:380-381`). There is no Low/iGPU baseline (§6, M0b).

## 1. Short answer

On Medium and High the game already draws at the panel's native resolution. The softness is not a
resolution cap, dynamic resolution or a post-processing pass, because the game has none of those.
Five causes remain. They are listed by how much each one is likely to explain:

1. **The 3D view cannot magnify.** With a fixed 70° vertical field of view, a burette's 0.1 mL marks
   are 0.9 device pixels apart on a held burette (the hold distance is a fixed 1 m) and about 1.5 px
   apart at 0.6 m. The texture holds the marks well, but the screen cannot show them. No texture or
   resolution fix can solve this. Only a narrower field of view or a closer view can (F1).
2. **Many label, screen, sign and ruler textures have 2–6× too few texels** for the distance they
   are read from, so the GPU magnifies them and they look blurred (F2).
3. **Most canvas textures have no anisotropic filtering** (anisotropy 1). Marks on curved or tilted
   surfaces smear at an angle (F3).
4. **Glass, liquids and metal have nothing to reflect** below High, and only 8 % of a reflection on
   High. Glass is drawn as 35 %-opaque light-blue paint, so it looks like frosted plastic, and every
   liquid except the titration flask is the same alpha-blended paint (F4–F6, F13).
5. **Small opaque apparatus barely casts a shadow.** The key light's shadow map is spread over a
   148° cone, about 17 mm per texel at bench height, wider than a 12 mm steel rod (F7). Glassware
   is a separate matter: glass and liquids **never** cast a shadow, by design, so no shadow-map
   size can ground a burette or a flask (F14).

A sixth cause is **suspected but not verified**. On a 150 % screen the canvas may be about half a
device pixel larger than its drawing buffer, so the browser would resample the whole frame (F8).
One console line confirms or rules it out (§6).

On **Low**, the frame is drawn at 1 pixel per CSS pixel and enlarged 1.5× by the browser. That is
blurry by design (F9). It matters only if the user was on Low, which nobody has confirmed yet (§6,
step M0).

## 3. How texels per pixel were computed

With vertical FOV θ = 70° (`config.ts:49`) and drawing-buffer height H, a surface facing the camera
at distance d gets this many device pixels per metre:

`p(d) = H / (2 · d · tan(θ/2)) = H / (1.4004 · d)`

| H (drawing-buffer height)              | d = 0.4 m | 0.6 m | 1.0 m | 2.0 m | 3.0 m |
| -------------------------------------- | --------: | ----: | ----: | ----: | ----: |
| 1402 (Medium/High, maximized, 150 %)   |      2503 |  1669 |  1001 |   501 |   334 |
| 1600 (Medium/High, full screen, 150 %) |      2857 |  1904 |  1143 |   571 |   381 |
| 935 (Low, maximized, 150 %)            |      1669 |  1113 |   668 |   334 |   223 |

A texture is sharp when its texel density is at least p(d), that is, at least 1 texel per pixel.
Below 1 it is magnified, and magnification is the blur the user sees.

**Viewing distances.** A held object is always 1 m from the eye: `player.holdDistance` defaults
to 1 m (`config.ts:25`) and is used as is by `InteractionSystem.ts:333` and `PhysicsSystem.ts:933`.
0.4 m is only the schema minimum, not a distance the player reaches. Bench apparatus is seen from
the capsule (radius 0.3 m) across the bench edge, probably 0.6 m or more; the real closest
eye-to-burette distance in the chemistry lab was **not measured** (§8, M9). The 0.4 m column is kept
only as a bound.

**Burette readability is limited by the screen, not by the texture.** One mL takes 9 mm of tube, so
0.1 mL takes 0.9 mm (`titration-stand.ts:58`). On the H = 1402 buffer at 70°:

| Distance               | 0.1 mL mark spacing | Low (H 935) |
| ---------------------- | ------------------: | ----------: |
| 1.0 m (held)           |              0.9 px |      0.6 px |
| 0.6 m (bench, assumed) |              1.5 px |      1.0 px |
| 0.4 m (bound only)     |             2.25 px |      1.5 px |

Below about 2 px per mark the marks cannot be told apart and form moiré. A human eye resolves about
1 arcminute; one screen pixel here is 70°/1402 ≈ 3 arcminutes. The view therefore shows about a
third of the eye's detail. The experiment's default reading resolution is 0.05 mL, "half a mark"
(`acid-base-titration.json:394-410`), and that reading is not resolvable on screen from any reachable
position. The recorded reading itself comes from the run's result through `simulation/measurement.ts`
(nearest multiple of d); the drawn meniscus and marks are presentation and are never a reading input.

### 5.1 Draw budget for the physical lab (estimate)

The frame is draw-bound (334–395 draws per view at 1280×720, docs/phase-lab-lighting-report.md §5).
Containers that the player moves cannot be merged into a rig. On Medium and up, one container costs
about five draws: glass filter + sheen (2), liquid filter + sheen (2) and a label (1); on Low about
three. A cabinet of 20–30 containers therefore adds about 100–150 draws (+25–45 %), plus shadow
draws for its opaque parts. Fix: draw **stored** containers instanced per type (spec §25 asks for
instancing) and give a container its own meshes only while it is out of storage; count draws per
container in the vertical slice (M3, cabinet view) before the chemistry migration goes on.

### 5.2 Per-tier switch path

The preset changes at once, mid-game (`Game.ts:641-646`, `ui/settings.ts:81-82`), but `applyQuality`
(`WorldRenderer.ts:766-779`) reaches only lights, shadows and the scene environment. F3 (anisotropy,
applied by three only on upload), F4/F13 (sheen on or off), F5/F6 (envMap, metalness) and F10 (a
different material class) live in 16 bench builders, the palette, the labels and `ExperimentView`.
Fix: a render-side registry. Builders register their reflective materials and canvas textures once,
at creation; on `GraphicsQualityChanged` the renderer walks it (never per frame), sets the tier's
parameters and `needsUpdate`, then warms the new programs (`warmDraw`). Unit test: switch
Low → High → Low and check every registered parameter. Stays in `src/platform`; no gameplay change.

### 5.3 Distance LOD (Lead decision)

The first version of this audit said "no LOD" because nothing is triangle-bound. That ignored the
user's explicit request and the fact that the frame is draw-bound: a distance LOD that **hides
draws** helps. Option: near (full), medium beyond about 3–4 m (no print, no label, no sheen pass),
far (body only). The distance is checked against the camera a few times a second or when the camera
moves, without allocation; a check costs far less than a draw. Measured with M3 in a far view of the
cabinet. `THREE.LOD` is not required; toggling `visible` on the sub-meshes is enough.

## 7. Decisions for the Lead

1. **Low/iGPU target and reachability (M0b).** Set Low's target as a number (for example "≥ N fps
   at 1699×935 on the 610M"). Recommendation: ask the user to try Windows graphics settings
   ("power saving" for Edge). If the 610M is still not used (the NVIDIA GPU drives the panel; a MUX
   or Advanced Optimus change is a system setting and a reboot, the user's call, never the agent's),
   record NOT RUN and enforce the cost-neutral rule for Low (§4 intro).
2. **Measurement method (M8).** Recommendation: uncapped A/B runs gate every cost decision; no
   number from a 300 Hz-capped run is used to accept or reject a sub-millisecond cost.
3. **Glassware grounding (F14) and shadow memory.** Recommendation: contact decals under vessels,
   one instanced draw per type, on Medium and up; decide Low after M0b. Ship High 2048 (~128 MiB)
   only after it is judged on the display. Ultra 4096 at ~512 MiB is not acceptable as is on an
   8 GB laptop GPU; reconsider it only if dark slots shrink (then about 128 MiB per lit key).
4. **Distance LOD (§5.3).** Recommendation: yes, draw-reducing visibility LOD for print, labels and
   sheen passes, added with the cabinet (when draws grow), measured with M3.
5. **Close-inspection view (F1).** Recommendation: yes, a held key, bound together with the pour,
   rotate and tilt controls of the physical lab so they do not compete for Mouse2 and the wheel; θ
   derived from the reading distance measured in M9 (expect about 18–30°); zoom as game state, with
   sensitivity scaled in PlayerSystem; HUD hint through `Texts` (EN+VI); unit test for the scaling
   and an e2e check of `camera.fov`. Without it, 0.1 mL marks stay unreadable on every tier.
6. **Liquid look (F13).** Recommendation: make it part of the PhysicalContainer visual contract now,
   so every migrated container gets it, colour and level read from the model's state only.
7. **The `quality.test.ts` materials guard (lines 146-147).** It forbids Physical materials and
   per-material envMaps in every tier. Recommendation: replace it with a per-tier guard (Low still
   has no envMap and no Physical material) plus the glassware-finish list test (F4).
8. **Ultra or not.** Decide after M8 and the High 2048 look.
9. **Frame-time target on the 300 Hz panel.** 300 fps leaves a 3.33 ms frame. The last native
   number (6c1: ANGLE RTX 5070 Laptop D3D11, 2548×1402, GPU 2.41 ms) is from 51 draws, before room
   lights; today's 334–395 draws were measured only at 1280×720 and capped (M8 explains why those
   timer values do not compare). The user should say whether High must hold 300 fps or whether
   144/165 is enough.

on first sight and applies the preset to whatever the renderer adds and, on a preset change, to
the whole scene; a switch Low → High → Low gives back exactly the builders' values
(`quality.test.ts`). The old global materials guard was replaced by a per-preset guard: no Physical

Cause: not the mipmaps (A2) nor the drawing (A3), but uploading a 2560×1520 (graph) and 1728×960
(status) canvas into an **sRGB** texture (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`): Chrome/ANGLE takes a CPU path for it,
~35 ms per repaint, once per repaint at 10 Hz and once per dial press. The same canvas into a plain
RGBA8 texture costs nothing measurable (A5). Fix (`ExperimentView.addScreen`): screens keep
1600 px/m on Medium and High; their texture is uploaded as RGBA8 (`NoColorSpace`) and decoded from
sRGB in the shader with three's own `DECODE_VIDEO_TEXTURE` define (three's workaround for video
for the same reason). Colours are the same; filtering and mipmaps now work on sRGB-encoded values
(as three's video textures do), invisible on a face-on screen but for the user's eyes. Low uses the
same path (cheaper, not measured). `quality.test.ts` checks the screen's format and define. Other
repainting canvas textures (digital microscope, incline-ramp tag) still upload as sRGB: not in this
bench, not measured.
